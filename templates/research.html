About This Research Study

WINK is being studied as a small, formative research pilot examining how an AI academic-support tool — grounded in a student's own, individually uploaded course materials rather than generic knowledge — is actually used by first-year and early-career college students, and how reliable its support turns out to be in practice. The pilot runs during the Fall 2026 semester (August 17 – December 20, 2026) at UTEP, with fewer than 10 student participants. Dr. Laura Trevino is the principal investigator.

Given the small number of participants, this is deliberately framed as an exploratory, formative pilot — its purpose is to surface usage patterns, feasibility, and reliability questions that a future, adequately powered study could test, not to establish a causal effect of WINK on grades, retention, or other outcomes. No claim of that kind is made from this pilot's data, and none should be inferred from the numbers reported elsewhere on this page.

Research Questions

Each question below links to where its data is actually reported further down this page.

  1. Adoption & engagement — Do first-year students actually adopt and sustain use of an AI academic-support tool once it's integrated with their real, individual course materials, and what does that usage look like over the course of a semester? (Measured via registration and session activity, feature usage across chat, documents, calendar, practice, and grades, and the behavioral engagement trends summarized on each student's own Progress page — organization, planning, help-seeking, general engagement, and practice activity, all based only on recorded behavior, deliberately without a single composite "success score," a risk prediction, or any causal claim attached to it.)
  2. Usage patterns — When given broad academic-support capabilities (deadline tracking, answering questions, generating practice material, calculating grades), what kinds of support do students actually seek out, and how does that differ across students and over time? (Measured via question clustering and feature-usage breakdowns computed from logged activity.)
  3. Answer reliability — How accurate are AI-generated answers when they're grounded in a student's own uploaded course materials, and do students correctly recognize when an answer is wrong? (Measured in "Answer provenance" and "Perceived helpfulness vs. faculty-rated correctness" below — the latter specifically checks for confident-sounding wrong answers a student approved of anyway, which matters more than a raw accuracy number on its own.)
  4. Deadline-extraction reliability — How reliably can deadlines be extracted automatically from real, unstandardized course syllabi, and how much correction do students need to make? (Measured in "Deadline extraction — confirmation status" below.)
  5. Faculty perspective — How do faculty evaluate the correctness and appropriateness of AI-generated academic support when shown a sample of real answers from their own or similar courses? (Measured via the faculty answer-rating tool at the bottom of this page, and the faculty-rated correctness figures used throughout.)

Participants & Consent

Participants are UTEP students who create a WINK account during the Fall 2026 pilot and agree to the research-data terms at registration, separately from WINK's operational Terms of Use. Consent is specific to this pilot: fewer than 10 students, one semester. A student can withdraw at any time by deleting their account, which immediately removes their name and email (replacing both with a non-identifying label) and disables their login; already-collected activity data is retained in that de-identified form to support the study rather than being deleted outright, consistent with the consent given at registration. Full detail is in the Privacy Policy and Terms of Use.
